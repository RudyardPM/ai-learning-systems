# AI Learning Mental Models

**A 4-minute self-assessment: when AI hands you an answer, what do you do next?**

👉 **[Take the assessment](https://claude.ai/artifact/DvfQsp2fDayKfgEkwbZ4g4)**

## The problem

Most AI training teaches the tool. Very little of it looks at the habit underneath: what a person instinctively does in the moment AI gives them something that looks finished. Some people check it. Some people ship it. Some people quietly stop practicing the skill the tool now handles for them.

That instinct predicts how well someone will keep learning as AI takes over more of their work, and most people have never noticed what theirs is. I wanted a fast, low-stakes way for people to see it.

## What it does

People work through 21 short, realistic scenarios ("An AI tool hands you a finished summary of a long report...") and pick their honest gut reaction. Each answer maps to one of seven mental models:

| Model | The habit |
| --- | --- |
| **The Auditor** | Verifies before trusting |
| **The Trainer** | Asks whether a tool is building capability or replacing it |
| **The Builder of Struggle** | Protects the friction that makes learning stick |
| **The Pilot** | Knows when to take the controls back |
| **The Cartographer** | Builds the map, not just the route |
| **The Chef** | Improvises when the plan breaks |
| **The Reviser** | Treats their own beliefs as a draft |

The results show the person's dominant model, their least-practiced model with a specific way to strengthen it, and a ranked profile of all seven. People can download their results.

## Design decisions

**Every option is a reasonable answer.** There's no wrong choice to avoid, so people answer from instinct instead of guessing what the test wants. That's what makes the result honest.

**Scenarios and answer order are shuffled every time.** Position bias is real. Randomizing both keeps the first or last option from winning by default.

**"Least practiced," not "weakness."** The bottom result is framed as the next place to grow, with one concrete practice attached. People act on a next step. They get defensive about a shortfall.

**Real situations, no jargon.** Each scenario is a moment people have already lived through: a colleague's AI-built recommendation, a skill that feels rusty, a workflow running fine for weeks. Nobody has to learn vocabulary before taking it.

**Skipping is allowed, with an honest warning.** If someone skips questions, the results say plainly that the read is rougher and suggest a retake.

**Each model rests on a simple metaphor.** Treating AI as an oracle versus auditing it. The GPS problem of reaching a destination without learning the route. Training wheels that never come off. Metaphors make the models easy to remember and easy to talk about as a team.

## How it connects

This is where the work starts. A team's spread of mental models shows which habits to build first. The [AI Product Coach](../pm-potential-ai-product-coach/) then practices those habits on real work: say what you believe, let AI push back, then make the call yourself.
